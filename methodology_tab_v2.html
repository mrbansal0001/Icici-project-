Decoding India's Micro-Wealth

The Affluence Index

India has no reliable income data below the district level. So instead of asking people what they earn, this index watches what wealthy neighbourhoods do — where they invest, where they shop, where they educate their kids, where they get treated when they're sick. 11 commercial and lifestyle signals, rolled up into a single 0–100 score per pincode, tell that story at a resolution income surveys never reach.

Why Proxies, Not Income

Every institution — a mutual fund distributor, a premium hospital, a golf club — runs its own feasibility study before opening a branch. They only show up where the economics work. A dense cluster of SEBI-registered investment advisors in a pincode isn't a guess about wealth; it's someone else's market research telling you the same thing. Stack enough of these independently-validated signals together, and the resulting picture of purchasing power is more current and more granular than any census or survey could be — updated in near-real-time by the market itself.

How the Score is Calculated

1

Signal Percentiling

Raw counts vary wildly — a corporate hub count of 0 to 500+ across ~19,300 pincodes. Rather than min-max or z-score scaling, every signal is converted to a percentile rank (0–100), which is far more robust to the extreme outliers and heavy skew found in things like vehicle counts. A score of 95 means: more of that signal than 95% of India's pincodes.

2

Pillar Averaging

Correlated signals are grouped into four pillars first, and only then averaged — so one underlying idea (say, "retail presence") can't accidentally get counted three or four times just because it happens to have three or four related columns feeding it.

3

Weighted Composite

The four Pillar Scores are combined into the final Affluence Score using weights that start equal at 25% each — a simple, defensible baseline that's easy to explain to a non-technical stakeholder. A PCA run on the standardized raw signals is used purely as a sanity check afterward, to flag if the data itself suggests a different emphasis — it never silently overwrites the business weights.

4

National Tiering

The composite score is ranked nationally and bucketed into agent-facing tiers: Platinum (top 2%), Gold (top 10%), Silver (top 30%), and Standard for everyone else.

The Four Pillars of Wealth

Financial Penetration

Tracks the density of wealth-management professionals. Investment advisors and mutual fund distributors set up shop strictly where investable surplus already exists — dense presence means a population that actively invests, which is exactly the demographic this index is built to find.

Commercial Vibrancy

Measures economic activity and corporate presence that drive high-income salaried employment and discretionary spending. The ratio of private-to-public bank branches is a particularly sharp cut here: private banks chase upper-middle-class density in a way public branches, which follow policy mandates, don't.

The 11 Signals Deep Dive

20 candidate signals were evaluated during research. 11 survived into the production model on data quality, coverage, and pincode-level resolution grounds; the rest were set aside — not because they're bad proxies in theory, but because they either fail to differentiate at this granularity or aren't reliably attributable to a single pincode.

Validation & Honesty

The Headline Result

State is never used as an input anywhere in the scoring pipeline — the model only ever sees pincode-level commercial and lifestyle signals. And yet, when you roll the resulting scores up by state, the ranking that falls out reconstructs India's real-world state-level wealth hierarchy almost exactly. That's the strongest evidence available that these 11 signals are capturing something real about economic geography, not noise or coincidence.

PCA Sanity Check

A principal component analysis is run on the standardized raw signals purely to check whether the natural variance structure of the data roughly agrees with equal 25% pillar weighting. It's diagnostic only — it flags when a rethink of weights might be warranted, but it never silently overrides the business logic.

Known-Pincode Face Validity

A small set of pincodes with independently well-known real-world affluence — premium urban cores expected to score very high, and remote rural pincodes expected to score low — are checked against the model's output as a manual gut-check before anything ships. This is a face-validity spot check, not a statistical proof, and is treated that way.

Limitations to Keep in Mind

Proxy, Not Ground Truth

This is a footprint-based estimate of purchasing power, not measured income. Two pincodes with identical scores can still have very different real income distributions underneath.

Sparse-Data Pincodes

Very low-population or newly-created pincodes can show noisy scores simply because there isn't enough commercial activity yet to differentiate them — low signal density isn't always the same as low affluence.

Equal Weights Are a Baseline

The 25%-per-pillar weighting is a defensible starting point, not a proven optimum. Treat tier boundaries as directional guidance for prioritization, not a precise ranking to the decimal point.
